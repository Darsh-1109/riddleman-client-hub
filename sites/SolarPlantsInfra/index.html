Investment Models

Choose a Solar Investment Model That Works for You.

CAPEX Model

Own your solar energy system.

  • Customer purchases the solar system outright.
  • Customer owns the installed asset completely.
  • Investment is made upfront.
  • Electricity savings accrue to the asset owner, subject to system performance and applicable tariffs.
  • Applicable depreciation benefits may be available to eligible businesses under prevailing tax rules.
Explore CAPEX Solutions

OPEX Model

Explore an alternative financing approach.

  • Alternative ownership and financing arrangements (e.g., RESCO).
  • Possibility of reduced or zero upfront investment.
  • Commercial arrangements depend on the specific project structure.
  • Payment and energy supply terms are contract-specific.
Explore OPEX Solutions

FAQ

Everything You Need to Know About Solar.

Solar panels absorb sunlight and convert it into direct current (DC) electricity. An inverter then converts this DC electricity into alternating current (AC), which is used by your home or business appliances. Any excess power can often be exported to the grid, depending on local regulations.
Yes. Industrial facilities typically have large, unused rooftop areas and high daytime electricity consumption, making them ideal candidates for solar energy. Solar can significantly reduce operational costs and provide predictable energy expenses.
In a CAPEX model, you purchase the solar system outright, own the asset, and receive all the electricity savings and depreciation benefits. In an OPEX model (like a RESCO arrangement), a third party may own and maintain the system, and you purchase the generated electricity at a predetermined tariff, often with little to no upfront cost.
As a general rule of thumb, approximately 100 square feet of shadow-free area is required to install 1 kW of solar capacity. However, the exact space needed depends on panel efficiency, mounting structure type, and site-specific shading.
Yes. Housing societies can install a common rooftop solar system to power common areas (like lifts, pumps, and lighting) or explore group captive models, subject to local electricity board regulations and society approvals.
Project costs depend on system size, component quality (panels, inverters), mounting structure type, rooftop accessibility, wiring distance, and any additional civil or structural reinforcement required.
Solar systems require minimal maintenance. Periodic cleaning of the panels to remove dust and debris, along with routine electrical inspections, is usually sufficient to ensure optimal performance.
Yes. By generating your own electricity, you draw less power from the grid, which directly reduces your monthly electricity bill. The exact reduction depends on system size, generation, and your consumption patterns.
EPC stands for Engineering, Procurement, and Construction. A turnkey EPC provider handles the entire project lifecycle: site assessment, system design, equipment sourcing, installation, grid coordination, and final commissioning.
